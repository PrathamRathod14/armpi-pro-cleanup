ArmPi Pro · blue cube fetch

Find the blue cube, pick it up, put it on the left

A Hiwonder ArmPi Pro looks around until its arm camera sees the blue cube, drives to it, picks it up and sets it down to its own left. Other cubes on the table are ignored: the robot steers only by where blue appears in the camera picture. The arm is placed over the cube with inverse kinematics, so it can grab the cube wherever it lies within reach; an arm-only mode keeps the wheels still.

The problem

Three things make this hard

One camera, on the arm

The only camera moves with the arm. The arm stays raised while the robot searches and drives, so the camera looks ahead. It only comes down to grab once the robot has stopped.

No distance sensor

Nothing measures distance. The robot reads it from the picture: a near cube looks bigger and sits lower. It stops when the cube reaches the spot recorded during calibration.

Other cubes in view

The red and wooden cubes look just as cube-like. Only the colour tells them apart, so detection keeps blue pixels and throws everything else away.

What the robot has

A camera that moves with the arm, and wheels that move any way

One USB colour camera, on the arm

Sees colours well but has no depth, so distance comes from the picture. It has crashed when its cable was pulled by arm moves, so the cable needs slack.

Six bus servos with position feedback

Base, shoulder, elbow, wrist, wrist turn and gripper. Arm positions are taught by hand and replayed. On this robot a low gripper value means open.

Mecanum wheels

Forward, backward, sideways and turning on the spot. Sideways slides do the final line-up without turning.

Raspberry Pi 4 with ROS Melodic

Runs the camera, servos and program. Big shoulder moves on a weak battery have cut its power, so moves are slow and the charger stays connected.

The arm stays in the raised Look pose for everything except the grab itself. The robot drives until the cube sits on the spot calibrated once by hand, then replays the taught grab.

Doing one thing at a time

The robot is always in exactly one state

The wheels and the arm never move at the same time.

The robot stops driving before the arm moves, and the arm finishes before the wheels move again. Every step is easy to watch and easy to stop with Ctrl+C, and a swinging arm never meets a moving floor.

If the blue cube leaves the picture for 2 seconds, the robot stops and searches again, up to three times. It never keeps driving toward where it thinks the cube was.

The camera picture in the raised pose. Far and off centre: turn toward it. In the band: drive forward. Near: slide until the cube covers the green cross, then grab.

The approach

See the cube, then let the arm maths put the gripper on it

Built on the robot's own inverse kinematics from Hiwonder's intelligent-grasp demo. No machine learning: colour vision plus a closed feedback loop.

1 · Find the cube by colour and shape

Each picture is converted to HSV and blue pixels are kept (bright enough to exclude navy clothing). A patch only counts as the cube if it is solid, fills its box and is not long and thin, and it must be seen in three pictures in a row. Its centre point comes from image moments.

2 · Point the gripper straight down

Inverse kinematics puts the gripper vertically above the table, 3 cm up. The camera on the gripper now looks at the table directly below, so the cube's position in the picture tells how far the gripper is from it.

3 · Chase the cube with IK

About ten times a second: cube left or right of the target point (320, 410) turns the base; cube ahead or behind changes the reach, from 12 to 25 cm, and IK recomputes the shoulder, elbow and wrist angles. It keeps following the same cube, not whatever is biggest.

4 · Grab, place, check

When the cube has stayed on the target point for ten pictures, IK lowers the gripper straight down and it closes. The cube goes to the taught drop position on the left. The camera then looks back: if the cube is still there, it tries again, up to eight times.

Working with the robot

Habits that save time and the robot

Fetch on the floor, hand on Ctrl+C

The robot cannot see edges or obstacles. Never run fetch on a table, and stop it the moment it does something unexpected.

Keep the charger connected

A weak battery has stopped the robot booting, and a big shoulder move has cut its power. Tap the shoulder keys rather than holding them.

Mind the camera cable

If the picture freezes after arm moves, the cable was probably tugged. Reseat it, leave slack for the low grab position, and reboot.

Change one setting at a time

Run, note what went wrong, change one value, run again. Two changes at once hide which one helped.

Re-check colours when the light changes

Colour detection depends on lighting. A different room or time of day means repeating task 4.

Halt before switching off

Run sudo halt and wait a few seconds before the power switch, so the SD card is not damaged.
