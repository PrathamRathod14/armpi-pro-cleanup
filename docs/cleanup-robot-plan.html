ArmPi Pro · blue and red cube fetch

Find the blue and red cubes, pick them up, put them in the basket

A Hiwonder ArmPi Pro turns until its front camera sees a blue or red cube, drives to it, lets its arm camera line up the grab, picks the cube up and drops it into the basket on its back, then looks for the next one until none is left. Other objects are ignored: the robot steers only by where blue or red appears in the pictures. It checks the cube is really in the gripper before going to the basket, and grabs again on the spot if it missed. The arm is placed over the cube with inverse kinematics, so it can grab the cube wherever it lies within reach; an arm-only mode keeps the wheels still. A YOLO-World model on the laptop can now find the object by name and send its position to the robot.

The problem

Three things make this hard

One camera, on the arm

The only camera moves with the arm. The arm stays raised while the robot searches and drives, so the camera looks ahead. It only comes down to grab once the robot has stopped.

No distance sensor

Nothing measures distance. The robot reads it from the picture: a near cube looks bigger and sits lower. It stops when the cube reaches the spot recorded during calibration.

Other cubes in view

The red and wooden cubes look just as cube-like. Only the colour tells them apart, so detection keeps blue pixels and throws everything else away.

What the robot has

A camera that moves with the arm, and wheels that move any way

One USB colour camera, on the arm

Sees colours well but has no depth, so distance comes from the picture. It has crashed when its cable was pulled by arm moves, so the cable needs slack.

Six bus servos with position feedback

Base, shoulder, elbow, wrist, wrist turn and gripper. Arm positions are taught by hand and replayed. On this robot a low gripper value means open.

Mecanum wheels

Forward, backward, sideways and turning on the spot. Sideways slides do the final line-up without turning.

Raspberry Pi 4 with ROS Melodic

Runs the camera, servos and program. Big shoulder moves on a weak battery have cut its power, so moves are slow and the charger stays connected.

The arm stays in the raised Look pose for everything except the grab itself. The robot drives until the cube sits on the spot calibrated once by hand, then replays the taught grab.

How it works

Six steps, from seeing blue to letting go

All six are in cleanup.py, run by python3 ~/cleanup.py fetch. Detection, calibration and the arm moves work on the robot; driving has run in short tests.

Doing one thing at a time

The robot is always in exactly one state

The wheels and the arm never move at the same time.

The robot stops driving before the arm moves, and the arm finishes before the wheels move again. Every step is easy to watch and easy to stop with Ctrl+C, and a swinging arm never meets a moving floor.

If the cube leaves the picture for 2 seconds, the robot stops and searches again, up to three times. It never keeps driving toward where it thinks the cube was.

The approach

See the cube, then let the arm maths put the gripper on it

Built on the robot's own inverse kinematics from Hiwonder's intelligent-grasp demo. No machine learning: colour vision plus a closed feedback loop.

1 · Find the cube by colour and shape

Each picture is converted to HSV and blue pixels are kept (bright enough to exclude navy clothing). A patch only counts as the cube if it is solid, fills its box and is not long and thin, and it must be seen in three pictures in a row. Its centre point comes from image moments.

2 · Point the gripper straight down

Inverse kinematics puts the gripper vertically above the table, 3 cm up. The camera on the gripper now looks at the table directly below, so the cube's position in the picture tells how far the gripper is from it.

3 · Chase the cube with IK

About ten times a second: cube left or right of the target point (320, 410) turns the base; cube ahead or behind changes the reach, from 12 to 25 cm, and IK recomputes the shoulder, elbow and wrist angles. It keeps following the same cube, not whatever is biggest.

4 · Grab, place, check

When the cube has stayed on the target point for ten pictures, IK lowers the gripper straight down and it closes. It lifts and the camera looks down again: a cube still lying on the floor looks the same size as before, a cube in the gripper looks much bigger. If it missed, it opens and grabs again on the spot, up to 3 times. Only then does the cube go into the basket on the robot's back. A final look after placing starts the whole attempt again if needed, up to eight times.

Laptop vision

YOLO-World on the laptop, grabbing on the robot

The Raspberry Pi is too slow for a modern detector, so the laptop does the seeing and the robot does the moving. Type a name such as "blue square" and the laptop finds it: no colour tuning, and it ignores clothing and shadows that fooled the colour filter.

Found by name

Seen from above the cube looks like a flat square, so the prompt "blue square" works and "blue cube" does not. The medium model is needed; the small one barely sees it.

Fresh pictures only

Each laptop result arrives about half a second after its picture. The robot moves in steps and only trusts a result from a picture taken after the arm stopped, so it never steers by an old view.

Nothing new on the robot

The laptop fetches pictures from the camera server and sends results through the ROS bridge, both already running on the robot.

Second camera: ACEBOTT AI vision module

The QD003 module is a K210 AI board with its own camera (GC2145) and screen, running CanMV. Over USB it is a serial port (/dev/ttyUSB0), not a webcam, so the robot never lists it as a camera; its screen works on any power. The robot talks to it (its startup messages were read on the robot). It does not clash with the arm, whose servos use /dev/ttyS0. Now used as the front camera: front_cam.py runs our own detection program on it and streams its picture.

Two cameras

The front camera finds, the arm camera grabs

The ACEBOTT AI vision module is mounted low on the front of the chassis. It looks for blue and red cubes on its own chip and tells the robot where the biggest one is; the arm camera then takes over for the precise grab.

front_cam.py

Stops ACEBOTT's program on the module (release the reset lines, wait about 6 s, repeated Ctrl-C), sends it a small CanMV program, and turns its output into ROS topics: /front_cam/image (the picture) and /front_cam/target (cube at x, y). Tries 1.5 Mbaud for smooth pictures and falls back to 115200. Nothing is saved on the module: switching it off and on brings ACEBOTT's program back.

Working with the robot

Habits that save time and the robot

Fetch on the floor, hand on Ctrl+C

The robot cannot see edges or obstacles. Never run fetch on a table, and stop it the moment it does something unexpected.

Keep the charger connected

A weak battery has stopped the robot booting, and a big shoulder move has cut its power. Tap the shoulder keys rather than holding them.

Mind the camera cable

If the picture freezes after arm moves, the cable was probably tugged. Reseat it, leave slack for the low grab position, and reboot.

Change one setting at a time

Run, note what went wrong, change one value, run again. Two changes at once hide which one helped.

Re-check colours when the light changes

Colour detection depends on lighting. A different room or time of day means repeating task 4.

Halt before switching off

Run sudo halt and wait a few seconds before the power switch, so the SD card is not damaged.
